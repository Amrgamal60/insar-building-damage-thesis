# Variogram check of the six nulls on the LiCSAR fields

**Plan:** `LICSAR_TEST_PLAN.md` (each null's variogram is re-checked on the LiCSAR field before its p-value is read). **Reproduce:** `.venv/bin/python scripts/17_licsar_test.py --check` (after 16). No model is fitted here.

- Same checks as `MSR_VARIOGRAM_CHECK.md`, on each test's LiCSAR field: semivariogram at lags 1–16 cell widths, Moran's I, skewness and variance of the surrogates against the observed field. The two permutation nulls keep no spatial structure by design and are not checked. Test 3 (random forest) uses the primary field and the first 500 of its surrogates.

## Reading (written after this check, before any p-value)

- **The LiCSAR field is smoother at short range than the Shirzaei field.** At 250 m its lag-1 semivariance is 0.37 (mm/yr)², 11% of its value at 1.5 km; Shirzaei's was 1.06, 26% (`MSR_VARIOGRAM_CHECK.md`). LiCSAR pixels are about 100 m and were multilooked and filtered before unwrapping, so neighbouring cells share signal and noise.
- **No null reproduces this at the shortest lags.** On the primary field the surrogates are rougher than the observed field: MSR at lags 1–3 (median 2.6×, 1.7× and 1.4× the observed), amplitude-adjusted MSR at lags 1–2, variogram-matching with the observed values at lags 1–2, and plain variogram-matching only at lag 1 (median 1.9×; the observed value is just below the 95% range). From 1 km on, every null is inside its range. The secondary fields show the same pattern, most strongly at 200 m, where MSR is outside at 9 of 16 lags.
- **What this means for the p-values:** a null that is rougher than the real field tends to produce fewer large chance gains, so its p-value tends to be too small. Plain variogram-matching is the closest match, so its p-value should be weighed most; the permutation nulls keep no spatial structure at all. The decision rule already uses the largest p of the six, so nothing in the plan changes.
