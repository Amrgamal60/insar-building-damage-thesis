## Short answers

### a) Unwrapping errors and closure-phase bias

- Standard correction tools: spatial bridging (islands of coherent pixels) and phase-closure (integer-ambiguity) correction in time, both in MintPy (Yunjun, Fattahi, Amelung 2019). Related: CorPhU (Benoit 2020, GJI), graph-theory plus compressed-sensing plus integer programming (Ma 2022, TGRS), iterative bridging plus closure (Oliver-Cabrera 2022, TGRS), and two 2025 ISPRS J. papers (Ma et al.; Liu et al.) that I could not read.
- Rates reported. The only quantified rate I read in full is Yunjun 2019's simulation: with a 5-connection network, phase-closure correction fully removes errors when fewer than about 20% of interferograms are affected. The maximum fully correctable input is 5%, 20% and 35% for 3, 5 and 10 connections. Mean output for a 20% input is about 2%. On a real Sentinel-1 case (Santiago island, Galapagos) 576 of 940 triplets were non-zero, the method did not fully correct, and a 0.5 cm/yr velocity bias remained. The authors also say the method can introduce new unwrapping errors. Benoit 2020 says "almost all detectable" errors were corrected in two case studies (abstract). No paper I read gives a residual-error rate per pixel for a building-scale analysis.
- Closure-phase (fading-signal) bias: Ansari 2021 (TGRS) shows a decaying short-lived phase term in multilooked short-baseline interferograms that biases deformation estimates and recommends dropping the shortest baselines. De Luca 2022 (TGRS comment) disputes this for the SBAS approach of Manunta 2019 and shows no significant bias in a Sicily test. Zheng 2022 (TGRS) finds differences of the order of cm/yr between time series built from different maximum temporal baselines (Barstow-Bristol Trough, CA). Falabella and Pepe 2022 (TGRS) propose mitigation. Maghsoudi 2022 (RSE) gives a linear-sum correction; per a search snippet of the paper, the bias differs by land cover (cropland and forest larger than urban). Biessel 2026 (TGRS) shows displacement heterogeneity inside the multilook window can itself create closure phase.
- OPERA DISP-S1: the producing paper is Staniewicz et al. 2026 (TGRS, arXiv 2511.12051). It uses sequential phase linking with compressed SLCs (mini-stacks of 15 SLCs), per-interferogram unwrapping (SNAPHU or EMCF) and an L1-norm network inversion "to limit propagation of unwrapping errors". Reference pixels come from the largest unwrapping connected component with temporal coherence above 0.95. The paper reports no residual unwrapping-error rate. It states L1 does not guarantee integer multiples of 2 pi, so regions or dates with large residuals should be masked. Validation: 12 frames, GPS double-difference test for station pairs within 50 km, 5 mm/yr 1-sigma requirement passed for 100% of the 8 applicable frames; mean velocity differences under 4 mm/yr at scales under 50 km. No atmospheric or ionospheric correction applied.
- EGMS (Costantini, Crosetto, Ferretti and others): the venue-filtered paper found is Crosetto et al. 2020 (Remote Sensing), which describes the service design. Validation material for EGMS that I found is conference or EGU abstracts (see outside-filter list). Nothing found in a Q1/Q2 or IEEE journal that reports unwrapping-error rates for EGMS.

### b) Noise level and detectability at a pixel or building

- Velocity uncertainty falls with time span. Fattahi and Amelung 2015 (JGR Solid Earth): uncertainty depends on delay standard deviation, number of acquisitions, time span and covariance with the reference; about 2 mm/yr over 100 km after ERA-I correction for 7 years of Envisat. Havazli 2021 (Sensors) simulates Sentinel-1 and finds about 1 mm/yr detection level only for regularly sampled records longer than 5 years (simulated range 3 to 10 years). Liu 2022 (Remote Sensing, Houston): uncertainty of "a couple of mm/yr" needs 5-year or longer records; RMS of detrended series sub-centimetre. Own note, labelled: none of these is a 1 to 2 year, single-pixel figure; the 24-month window in D3 is shorter than any record they quantify.
- Accuracy against ground truth: Cigna 2021 (Remote Sensing): two years of Sentinel-1, standard deviation of vertical velocity differences 9 to 10 mm/yr against GNSS and 8 mm/yr against levelling in a fast-subsiding valley, with larger discrepancies where benchmarks sit within the same pixel as a fault.
- Building scale: Talib 2022 (RSE) is the only building-level study found: TerraSAR-X (25 cm to 1 m resolution, 1.7 to 2.5 years) found subsidence of 3 to 6 mm/yr at buildings of 300 to 2,000 m2 linked to sinkhole activity. Alizadeh 2024 (Land) compares Sentinel-1 (144 images) with TerraSAR-X (36 images) at collapse sites: time series strongly correlated, X-band found extra collapse-prone points.
- Nothing found that states a minimum detectable velocity difference between one building and its neighbours for Sentinel-1, and nothing that compares persistent and distributed scatterers specifically in low-rise residential areas.

### c) Precursor, change-point and DL anomaly methods

- Published validation is thin. Kulshrestha 2022 (JSTARS): bidirectional LSTM, Ireland, 104 images; about 99% accuracy for linear points but at most 62% for anomalous Heaviside or Breakpoint classes. Shakeel 2022 (ALADDIn, TGRS): 91.25% on a synthetic case plus one real M5.7 earthquake. Lattari 2022 (TGRS): LSTM change-point detector validated on simulated data because ground truth is lacking, plus an application in Tuscany. Fontana 2024 (Remote Sensing): functional data analysis, one mud-volcano eruption. Milillo 2022 (Frontiers in Earth Science): supervised network on 170 million Italian time series from ERS, ENVISAT, COSMO-SkyMed, compared with independently validated data. Bull. Eng. Geol. Environ. 2023: LSTM forecasting, three fatal mining incidents.
- No abstract I read reports false-alarm rates on stable areas or a count of independent ground-truth events above a handful. Hill 2021 (JGR Solid Earth) is the key cautionary result: on 2,000 random Sentinel-1 points, simple constant extrapolation beat SARIMA and LSTM overall, and the authors state limits for "nonperiodic signals or individual measurement points".
- Stated limits are mostly about site dependence and small numbers of events (see table).

### d) Seasonal and thermal signal modelling

- Seasonal shrink-swell: Liu 2022 (Houston) compares InSAR and GPS seasonal amplitudes (below 4 mm in non-subsiding areas, over 6 mm in subsiding areas, comparable between the two). Burnol 2021 and Toure 2025 are already assessed and not repeated.
- Thermal dilation: Qin 2018 (Remote Sensing, X-band) maps thermal dilation of three infrastructure types and states the difficulty is separating it from residual topographic phase and trend deformation. Martin 2022 (JSTARS) applies blind source separation to MT-InSAR to separate thermal and ground components of bridges and buildings; validated on synthetic and real data. Both address large structures; nothing found on thermal dilation of detached houses with Sentinel-1.
- Seasonal fitting also appears in Hill 2021 (sinusoid fitting, seasonal decomposition).

### e) Reference frame and long-wavelength errors

- Ionosphere: Liang 2019 (TGRS) finds C-band ionospheric effects strong for low-latitude ascending tracks and weak or negligible elsewhere (mid-latitude and low-latitude areas tested). Wang 2026 (TGRS): GNSS-TEC correction lowers RMSE against GNSS from 1.7 to 3.8 down to 0.9 to 2.9 mm/yr for Sentinel-1 ascending tracks in mid-latitudes and all descending tracks; less effective for low-latitude ascending tracks. Piromthong 2026 (TGRS) time-series ionospheric mitigation and unwrapping-error detection. Marconato 2025 (GRL): global ionosphere maps capture seasonal and multi-year ramps with amplitudes of 0.2 mm/km, after correcting solid Earth tides and ocean loading; relevant for scales over 100 km.
- Tides: Yu 2020 (GRL): ocean tide loading largest in coastal regions; correction cut time-series standard deviation by 33% (troposphere only) to 52% (troposphere plus loading) in southwest Britain.
- Troposphere: Bekaert 2015 (RSE) compares correction methods; Fattahi 2015 above. OPERA DISP-S1 applies none of these and is referenced to a pixel near the frame centroid, not calibrated to GNSS (Staniewicz 2026).
- Own note, labelled: for the 1 to 20 km comparisons in the study, the sources above quantify errors mostly at 50 to 400 km. I found no paper that quantifies these terms specifically at 1 to 20 km.
