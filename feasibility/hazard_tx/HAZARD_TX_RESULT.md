# Result: confirmatory external test of a published InSAR differential-settlement hazard layer against recorded foundation repairs in San Antonio and Dallas (2022–2025)

- **Plan:** `HAZARD_TX_PLAN.md`, frozen 2026-11-22 (`freeze/HAZARD_TX_PLAN_frozen.md`, SHA-256 `d6f25de61ac85dd8…`).
- **Deviations (§14):** one, procedural. The event tables were built on the PC, not the server, so that raw permit text stayed in the private area. Only the tables (house id, dates, status) went to the server, with hashes checked there. No rule, input or output changed.
- **Run:** `scripts/run_ht.py real` on the server, 2026-11-22 23:55:44 to 2026-11-23 02:25:04 EET, with 96 workers. Output `real_20261122/real/results.json` (SHA-256 `8444aacf81753c2d…`). The code hashes recorded in it match `freeze/MANIFEST.sha256`.

## 6. Interpretation

*Written 2026-11-23, after the real result was seen. Every number below is read from `results.json`.*

**Answer to the prespecified question (plan §8).** The reading is **"Added information demonstrated, enrichment below δ"**. The hazard flag (β classes 2–3) was associated with more first foundation-repair permits beyond the flexible Baseline 1: pooled adjusted IRR 1.25 (90% 1.09 to 1.47), one-sided p 0.0077. It also gave a positive held-out gain (T +11.93). Under the frozen rule, this means the published layer adds held-out information about which houses receive a foundation-repair permit. The 90% upper bound (1.47) is below δ = 1.5, the smallest enrichment the plan judged worth acting on as a screen. So an enrichment of that size is excluded at the level the test uses.

**What the result rests on.**

- **San Antonio carries the result.** It holds 850 of the 863 flagged events. Its T is +13.48, positive in 11 of 11 fold assignments, and its adjusted IRR is 1.25 (95% 1.05 to 1.55).
  - Dallas has 1,130 flagged houses and 13 flagged events. Its crude rates are flat (2.91 vs 2.94 per 1,000 house-years), its T is -1.55 (0 of 11 positive), and its adjusted IRR is 1.29 (95% 0.48 to 2.15).
  - Plan §11 expected Dallas to be uninformative on its own. It is: it neither supports nor contradicts the pooled estimate.
  - The ratio of IRRs (Dallas / San Antonio) is 1.03 (95% 0.42 to 1.79). This does not show heterogeneity, but it cannot exclude it.
  - So this is a San Antonio finding inside a pooled test. It is not a replication in two cities.
- **The gain is in fit, not in ranking.** T is an out-of-fold log-likelihood gain. Ranking measures barely move: in San Antonio, ROC AUC changes by +0.0001 and PR AUC by -0.0001.
  - The flag marks 5.0% of houses and holds 12.2% of events. That concentration is unadjusted (pooled IRR 2.29), and the baseline already captures most of it (adjusted 1.25).
  - These data give no support to a claim that the layer improves screening or targeting beyond the baseline.
- **T is less certain than the IRR test.** The block bootstrap of pooled T runs from -9.5 to +60.0, and 15.0% of resamples are at or below 0. Under a planted IRR of 1.5, 5.5% were (plan §7).
  - The §8 reading uses the point value of T, as prespecified. The interval is descriptive.

**Descriptive caveats from the secondaries** (nominal; no claim beyond §8):

- **Within neighbourhoods (secondary 6).** With 2 km block fixed effects, the IRR falls to 1.13 (95% 0.97 to 1.39; p 0.0553).
  - Part of the adjusted excess may be neighbourhood-level, such as differences in recording, contractor activity or income beyond the tract, rather than a difference between nearby houses.
  - These data cannot separate that from the loss of contrast when flagged houses cluster inside blocks.
- **Baseline choice (secondary 16).** The linear Baseline 1 gives 1.44 (95% 1.07 to 2.10) against 1.25 under the flexible one. The planted check found the linear baseline anti-conservative (plan §9.2: nonlinear soil gave 23% false demonstrations).
  - The Austin lead (1.74) used the linear baseline. Compared like for like: San Antonio 1.44 and Dallas 1.36.
  - The three-city summary (1.53, secondary 12) also uses the linear baseline. It is not the estimate of this study.
- **Vertical land motion (secondary 5)** points the other way. Per 1 mm/yr of subsidence, the IRR is 0.84 unadjusted and 0.94 adjusted. Austin showed the same direction (0.79; plan, design summary).
  - The information found here is in the layer's differential-settlement (angular-distortion) classes, not in the subsidence rate near the house. This study does not explain the direction.
- **Robustness of the primary estimate:**
  - other inferences (secondary 13): r1 1.25, p 0.0004396; r2 1.25, p 0.0006; r3 1.25, p 0.0042;
  - San Antonio houses with a recent construction permit removed (secondary 15): 1.24;
  - Dallas broader outcome (secondary 17): 1.25;
  - houses near a flag edge excluded (secondary 10): 1.34, T +23.40;
  - the map user's domain (secondary 9): 1.32;
  - trend per hazard class (secondary 3): 1.24;
  - the risk flag (classes 3–4, secondary 4) is weaker: 1.14, T +4.50.
- **By year (secondary 8):** 2022 1.22 (p 0.0759); 2023 1.17 (p 0.1247); 2024 1.14 (p 0.1307); 2025 1.45 (p 0.0063). Years with nominal p below 0.05: 1 of 4.
- **Demolition as the outcome (secondary 11):** 0.93.
- **Class 3 is almost absent** (47 San Antonio houses; 0 in Dallas). In practice the flag is class 2.

**Scope.**

- The outcome is a permit: an owner's decision to repair, not verified damage. Nothing here is a causal statement about ground motion and foundations.
- The result applies inside the map's domain only (InSAR-coherent areas; flow in §2).
- For the thesis question: in this frozen test, a published InSAR-derived hazard product added a small amount of held-out information about recorded foundation repairs, almost entirely in San Antonio.
  - The enrichment was below the size the plan set as useful (δ).
  - It did not improve ranking.
